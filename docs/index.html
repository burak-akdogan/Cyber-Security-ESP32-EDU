STUDENT RESPONSIBILITY

Ethical Code of Conduct

Cybersecurity professionals have powerful tools. With that power comes responsibility. These classroom labs are designed to help you understand how attacks work, how systems can be defended, and how security professionals think.

🎯

1. Get Permission First

I will only test systems, networks, devices, and accounts when I have explicit permission to do so. A classroom lab is authorized because the instructor has provided the environment for the activity.

🧪

2. Stay Inside the Lab

I will use ESP32 devices and cybersecurity tools only within the designated classroom environment. I will not target school, personal, public, or neighboring networks or devices.

🔒

3. Protect Privacy

I will not intentionally collect, expose, save, or share passwords, private messages, personal information, device identifiers, or other sensitive data.

🧠

4. Understand Before You Attack

The goal of an attack simulation is learning. Before running a tool, I should understand what it does, what it could affect, and how a defender could detect or prevent it.

🛡️

5. Think Like a Defender

For every offensive technique, I will ask: What is the risk? How could this attack be detected? How could a system be protected?

🚫

6. Stop When Something Goes Wrong

If a lab behaves unexpectedly, affects another device, or leaves the authorized environment, I will stop immediately and notify the instructor.

📚

7. Document Responsibly

I will document what I tested, what happened, and what I learned. Reports should focus on understanding vulnerabilities and improving security rather than demonstrating unauthorized access.

🤝

8. Respect Other Students

I will not interfere with another student's device, network traffic, lab, or project. Cybersecurity learning should be collaborative, controlled, and respectful.

⚡ My Cybersecurity Commitment

I understand that cybersecurity tools can be used for both defensive and harmful purposes. I will use the skills I learn in this course responsibly, only with authorization, and with the goal of protecting people, systems, networks, and information.

Think. Get permission. Test safely. Protect others. Learn.
